model

Each face has two moisture stores, stepped hourly from 14 days back:
a surface film that rain and condensation fill and evaporation empties, and a slow
pore store that soaks up some rain, drains over a few days and wicks back to the
surface (why grit stays green and greasy after a wet week).
Evaporation is Penman for a wet surface, driven by sunlight on the actual face
(vertical plane at the crag's aspect, sun position by hour), wind at the face (reduced by shelter)
and vapour-pressure deficit. Rock temperature lags air temperature and warms in sun;
when the dew point is above rock temperature it condenses (“sweating” grit).



friction — dry-rock grip from rock temp, humidity, dew point and breeze.
cold, dry and breezy = prime; warm, humid, still or sweating = greasy.



rock — damage risk. wet: wet grit is weaker and holds/flakes snap — don't.
saturated: surface may be dry but the pores are still full after prolonged rain
(or wet rock near freezing); go gentle on flakes, soft holds and anything friable.



caveats — v1, hand-tuned, not yet calibrated against observations.
It doesn't know about green streaks, seepage lines or a single damp route on an otherwise dry crag.
Forecast skill drops after ~3 days; by day 5–7 treat it as a trend.
